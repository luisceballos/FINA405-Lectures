Use 1 · Transform an existing liability

An FRN resets the coupon, not the spread.

  • The borrower pays SOFR + the contractual spread.
  • For our examples, the rate observed at t=0 sets the payment at t=0.5.

All teaching rates are annual, with semiannual payments. SOFR is fixed at the start of each semester for these examples. Actual SOFR contracts can use overnight compounding and different day-count conventions. Interest calculations exclude principal repayment.

Takeaways · Two main uses of an IRS

Two financing decisions. One cash-flow method.

1. Transform an existing liability

Slides 1–6

Keep the original debt and use a swap to change its interest-rate exposure: floating to fixed, or fixed to floating.

Goal: match the company’s desired exposure and cash-flow predictability.

Our example: SOFR + 1% debt becomes 5% fixed, or $50 per semester on $2,000. A net swap payment does not mean the hedge failed.

2. Reduce the cost of new borrowing

Slides 7–10

Compare direct fixed and floating borrowing rates. Borrow in one market, then swap into the desired exposure.

Goal: achieve a lower all-in rate than borrowing directly in the preferred market.

Our A/B example: 7.5% fixed versus 8%, and SOFR + 2.5% versus SOFR + 3%. Each saves 0.5 percentage points under the given terms.

Always evaluate debt and swap together. Cost savings depend on the borrowing quotes, swap terms and fees; they are not guaranteed by using an IRS.
