Section 1 — Basics
What is a Bond?
A bond is a loan — the issuer borrows from investors and promises fixed cash flows.
Key Components
Face Value (FV) — amount repaid at maturity (usually $1,000)

Coupon Rate — annual interest rate as % of FV

Maturity — when FV is repaid (1yr to 30yr+)

Price — what investors pay today (≠ FV)

Section 1 — Yield to Maturity
Price ↔ Yield: The Inverse Relationship
YTM Formula (Semi-Annual)
YTM is the single discount rate that makes PV of all cash flows equal to the current price.
Why the Inverse Relationship?
If yields ↑ (new bonds pay more), old bonds become less attractive → price ↓

If yields ↓ (new bonds pay less), old bonds are more valuable → price ↑
Interactive: 10yr Bond · 8% Coupon · $1,000 FV
YTM: 8.0% Price: $1,000
Section 1 — Example 1
YTM Calculation: Premium Bond
Bond trades above par — what does that tell us about yields?
Given Data
Price: $1,200
Face Value: $1,000
Coupon Rate: 15% per year (semi-annual)
Maturity: 2 years
Periods: n = 4 (semi-annual)
Semi-annual coupon = 15% × $1,000 / 2 = $75
Excel / Calculator
=RATE(4, 75, −1200, 1000) × 2
= 4.34% per year
Interpretation
Price ($1,200) > FV ($1,000)
→ Trading at a premium
→ YTM (4.34%) < Coupon Rate (15%)

High coupon relative to market rates makes this bond expensive.
Section 1 — Example 2
YTM Calculation: Deep Premium Bond
Same coupon, longer maturity — how does time affect YTM?
Given Data
Price: $1,500
Face Value: $1,000
Coupon Rate: 15% per year (semi-annual)
Maturity: 10 years
Periods: n = 20 (semi-annual)
Semi-annual coupon = 15% × $1,000 / 2 = $75
Excel / Calculator
=RATE(20, 75, −1500, 1000) × 2
= 7.66% per year
Compare to Example 1
Longer maturity → higher YTM (7.66% vs 4.34%)
Price ($1,500) still > FV → still a premium bond
YTM (7.66%) still < Coupon Rate (15%)

More periods to discount → time value of money matters more.
Section 2 — The Fed
Who Controls Interest Rates?
The Federal Reserve (The Fed)
The Fed sets the Federal Funds Rate — the overnight rate at which banks lend to each other.

This rate cascades through the entire economy: T-bill rates, mortgage rates, corporate bond yields, savings accounts.

Section 2 — Benchmark Rate
T-Bonds: The Risk-Free Benchmark
Every yield in finance is quoted as a spread over the "risk-free" Treasury rate.
Why "Risk-Free"?
US Treasury bonds are backed by the full faith and credit of the US government.

The US has never defaulted on its debt. Investors treat T-bond yields as the minimum return they will accept for any investment.
The Fundamental Equation
Any borrower with credit risk must pay more than the risk-free rate. The extra amount is the credit spread.

Section 3 — Credit Risk
Credit Spreads: Pricing Default Risk
Investors demand extra return for the possibility of non-payment.
The Basic Relationship
The credit spread compensates investors for:
• Probability of default (PD)
• Loss given default (LGD)
• Illiquidity premium
Mortgage Rates — A Real Example
30yr fixed mortgage ≈ 10yr T-bond + ~250–300bp

When T-bond rate rises 50bp → mortgage rate also rises ~50bp
→ Higher monthly payments for homebuyers

Section 3 — Case Study
Evergrande: When Spreads Warned First
China's largest property developer defaulted in 2021 — bond markets saw it coming.
The Collapse
Evergrande Group — Chinese real estate giant with over $300B in liabilities.

By mid-2021, its bonds traded at 20–30 cents on the dollar, implying a massive credit spread (2,000+ bp).

December 2021: Official default declared. Creditors recovered little.
The Timeline of Distress
2019: Bonds trade near par, spread ~500bp
2020: Spread widens to ~1,000bp (warning)
Mid-2021: Spread >2,000bp — markets signal crisis
Late 2021: Default. Bonds at $0.20 on $1.00
Key Lesson
Bond markets are forward-looking.
Credit spreads embed the market's assessment of default risk — often before rating agencies act.

⚠ Evergrande was still rated B+ by Fitch in Jan 2021

The spread explosion to 2,000+ bp told bond investors to exit months before the rating agencies downgraded.

In fixed income: watch spreads, not just credit ratings.

Section 3 — Ratings
Credit Rating Agencies
Moody's, S&P, and Fitch translate credit analysis into a letter grade.
The Rating Scale
Moody'sS&P / FitchMeaning

Aa1–Aa3AA+–AA−Very high quality

Baa1–Baa3BBB+–BBB−Investment grade floor
Ba1–Ba3BB+–BB−Speculative / "Junk"
B1–B3B+–B−High risk
Caa–CCCC–CDistressed / near default

Why Ratings Matter
Many institutional investors (pension funds, insurance) are mandated to hold only investment-grade (BBB− / Baa3 or above).

A downgrade below BBB− — a "fallen angel" — forces forced selling, widening spreads dramatically.
Limitations of Ratings
• Ratings are opinions, not guarantees
• Backward-looking (accounting data)
• Conflicts of interest (issuer pays)
• Slow to react (2008 crisis, AAA-rated CDOs defaulted)

What We Learned — Fixed Income
What We Learned Today
1
YTM is the key metric. It's the single discount rate that prices all future cash flows. When price rises, YTM falls — the inverse relationship is fundamental.
2
The Fed sets the baseline. Every rate in the economy is anchored to the federal funds rate. When the Fed moves, mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs, and bond prices all adjust.
3
T-bonds are the risk-free benchmark. Every rate in finance is anchored to the Treasury yield. Corporates and sovereigns pay a spread above it — the higher the credit risk, the wider the spread.
4
Credit spreads price default risk. i = rf + credit spread. Evergrande's spreads blew out to 2,000bp before the actual default — bond markets are forward-looking.
5
Ratings are a guide, not a guarantee. Agencies provide a useful baseline but lag the market. Real-time credit spreads are the best live signal of investor sentiment.
6
Apple trades near risk-free. At only 84bp over Treasuries, markets treat Apple (AA+) as nearly default-proof — a perfect illustration of how credit spreads reveal perceived issuer quality.
